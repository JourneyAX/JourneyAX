# JourneyAX launch film — motion-graphics cut v2

## The one-sentence story

Customers arrive with a goal, but a merchant's answers and actions are scattered across systems. JourneyAX connects those systems into usable context, lets the merchant define agents and a Business Pack, and guides each customer through an adaptive journey on the merchant's own channel.

**Format:** 2:57, 16:9, silent master, 1280×720 authored frames encoded as 1920×1080 H.264 at 20 fps, plus twelve separate 12–18 second animated clips. This is an animated concept film, not a product-recording or a claim that every connector and use case is deployed. No narration is required; each clip must make sense without a paragraph of explanation.

## What each term means in this film

- **Connections:** approved access to PIM/catalogue, ERP, CRM, commerce, search and knowledge. Some content can be indexed for discovery; volatile price, stock, customer and order facts should be requested from their authoritative systems when needed. The diagram means unified access and context, **not** one giant data copy.
- **Agent:** a merchant-defined specialist with a purpose, approved knowledge and tools, limits, and a model policy. The merchant can build several roles, such as discovery, product guidance and quotation.
- **Business Pack:** the merchant's controlled playbook: brand voice, knowledge references, agent definitions, journeys, rules, capabilities, cards and themes. It configures behaviour and experience; it is **not** the live ERP, CRM or inventory database.
- **Journey:** the customer-facing progression. It begins with the goal, remembers context, branches only when a detail is needed, invokes an appropriate agent or action, and presents a card, answer, plan, quote, basket or handoff when useful. It is not a fixed questionnaire.
- **Go and Studio:** Go is the shopper experience; Studio is the merchant's control surface for connections, agents, journeys, packs and insights. The screens are stylised illustrations.

## Production and claim rules

1. Avoid a slide deck aesthetic. Each shot needs an action: data signals travelling, UI state changing, a path branching, a card appearing only when relevant, or a camera move through a human context. Use the supplied MP4 as the motion-graphics first cut, not as the final agency edit.
2. Maintain JourneyAX colors: `#070707` black, `#101010` raised dark, `#FFFDF6` warm white, `#FFD600` signal yellow. Use the authentic supplied logo. Do not generate a replacement wordmark.
3. No made-up SKUs, prices, inventory levels, customer profiles, conversion charts or claims that all six connectors are live. Keep “illustrative” labels and a separate production fact-check before distribution.
4. The real differentiation is merchant ownership of the customer channel and a continuous journey from need to outcome. Do not lead with model-provider names or vague “AI magic.”
5. Do not imply that the Business Pack stores live ERP/CRM/stock facts. The Pack defines the merchant's behaviour and presentation; connectors retrieve authorised facts and perform approved actions.
6. The three examples demonstrate a general platform pattern. Do not hardcode these use cases as the only possible journeys.

## Copy/paste prompt for a motion designer or video-generation agent

Use the supplied `journeyax-launch-v2-silent.mp4`, the twelve short clips, scene stills, original photographic assets, authentic JourneyAX logo and this brief to create a premium launch film. The existing MP4 is an animated motion-graphics rough cut, not the final visual treatment. Preserve the 2:57 story and exact conceptual architecture. Make the architecture feel alive: source systems at left, JAX at centre, customer and merchant outcomes at right, with unmistakable data signals travelling along the paths. Add 3D depth/parallax, purposeful camera moves and staged UI reveals; do not make it look like a slide deck. Distinguish the Business Pack (merchant playbook) from live source systems. Show three human customer stories: building supplies project, manufacturing component configuration, and apparel fit. Keep the customer experience and Studio control surface conceptual, clearly labelled illustrative. Each short clip should communicate one action with a brief headline, not a paragraph. No voice-over. No fabricated screenshots, metrics, prices, stock, integrations or specific product claims. Export a 1080p silent H.264 master, an editable project, and short standalone clips. If licensed music is later added, retain the silent master.

## How to turn this into a public launch asset

1. Review the rough cut for narrative and positioning. This is the fast point to change message or sequence.
2. Replace any conceptual Studio/Go UI that should be presented as a real feature with **current approved product capture**, or retain an explicit “illustrative” tag. Confirm connector, agent-building and action claims with the product owner.
3. Animate each of the three photos as short moving shots (subtle camera push, depth/parallax, no synthetic speaking faces). Animate connecting paths and UI changes continuously, not via crossfades alone.
4. Add licensed instrumental sound design if desired, but no voice. Do a sound-off readability pass on laptop and mobile; shorten lines rather than shrinking type.
5. Export final 16:9 master and vertical/social cutdowns. Do legal/brand approval before public distribution.
